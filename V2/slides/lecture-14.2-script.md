> Delivery marks: **bold** = stress · / = brief pause · [pause] = 1 second · [long pause] = 2 to 3 seconds. ▶ CLICK = advance to the next build. "Transition:" is the spoken bridge into what comes next.

## 1. The last 48 hours and exam day

(About 40 seconds)

This is the last lecture of the course. [pause] You've done the hard part. / Now it's about turning up rested, / calm, / and with a plan for every question. [pause]

If I had to sum it up in one sentence, / it would be this. [pause] **Rest** before the exam, / and on the day, / find the **constraint** first. [long pause]

Transition: So, / what do the last forty-eight hours look like?

## 2. Review less, rest more

(About 1 min 30 seconds)

The last two days are for sharpening, / not for learning new things. [pause]

▶ CLICK

**Forty-eight hours out**, / review your weakest domains. / Go back to the checkpoint questions you got wrong, / and make sure you know **why** the right answer is right.


▶ CLICK

**Twenty-four hours out**, / run the "Which service?" drill / and the look-alike traps one more time. / Nothing new. / New topics the day before only add doubt.


▶ CLICK

**The evening before**, / stop. / Lay out your ID, / check your booking, / and get a proper night's **sleep**.


▶ CLICK

And **exam morning**. / Eat something, / skip the last-minute cramming, / and arrive, / or check in, / early. [pause] A rested brain reads constraint words much better than a tired one. [long pause]

Transition: Now, / the practical side. / Where and how you sit the exam.

## 3. Two ways to sit the exam

(About 1 min 30 seconds)

You book the exam through **Pearson VUE**, / and you can sit it in two ways. [pause]

▶ CLICK

The first is **at a test centre**. / Arrive early, / with your ID. / Your phone and your bag go in a locker, / and you sit at a prepared computer.


▶ CLICK

The second is **online, / from home**. / Run the Pearson VUE system test a few days before, / on the same computer and network you'll use. / You need a quiet room / and a clear desk. / And check in early, / because you'll take photos of yourself, / your ID / and the room before you start.


▶ CLICK

And one thing catches people out. [pause] The name on your ID must **match** the name on your certification account. / Check it now, / not on the morning of the exam. [long pause]

Transition: Once you're sitting down, / the next thing that matters is time.

## 4. About 75 questions in about three hours

(About 1 min 45 seconds)

You'll get about **seventy-five** questions, / in about **three hours**. / Your booking confirmation shows the exact time you get, / so check it there. [pause] That's plenty of time, / as long as you don't get stuck. [pause]

▶ CLICK

So, / work in two passes. / On the **first pass**, / answer every question you can, / and flag the ones you're unsure about.


▶ CLICK

Use the **two-minute rule**. / If a question has taken two minutes and you're still stuck, / pick your best guess, / flag it, / and move on.


▶ CLICK

Then, / on the **second pass**, / go back to your flags, / with all the time you saved.


▶ CLICK

And **never** leave a blank. / There's no penalty for guessing, / so a blank is the only answer that's always wrong. [long pause]

Transition: So, / what do you actually do with each question?

## 5. Four steps to the answer

(About 1 min 45 seconds)

Here's a method you can use on every single question. [pause]

▶ CLICK

One. / **Read the last sentence first**. / The scenario can be long. / The last sentence tells you what's actually being asked.


▶ CLICK

Two. / **Find the constraint**. / Lowest cost. / Least operational effort. / Real time. / Most secure. / No code changes. / That phrase is what separates the right answer from the almost-right one.


▶ CLICK

Three. / **Cross out two options**. / Usually two are clearly wrong. / The wrong service, / the wrong layer, / or they break the constraint.


▶ CLICK

And four. / **Pick the one that meets every constraint**. / And if two answers both work, / the managed option, / with less for you to build and run, / is usually the one they want. [long pause]

Transition: And then, / you press submit. / So, / what happens next?
